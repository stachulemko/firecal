FireCal

Harmonized burning activity calendar · MODIS + VIIRS active fire hotspots since 2000

Any place on Earth: pick a country or region, click it on the map, or draw a rectangle.

Pick an area and press “Analyse area”

FireCal downloads every active-fire hotspot NASA FIRMS has for the area, converts MODIS (1 km, 2000–) and VIIRS (375 m, 2012–) detections into one consistent record, and builds a day-by-day burning calendar, its normal pattern, unusual episodes and a 14-day forecast.

The first run of a new region downloads the yearly archives (a few minutes); later runs use the cache.

How FireCal works

  1. Area. Any country or territory of the world, any state or province of the nine largest countries, or a drawn rectangle. Only the FIRMS country archives the area needs are downloaded.
  2. Data. NASA FIRMS active-fire hotspots: MODIS Terra/Aqua Collection 6.1 (1 km, Nov 2000–), VIIRS S-NPP (375 m, 2012–), NOAA-20 (2018–) and NOAA-21 (2024–) from the public yearly country archive, the keyless 7-day near-real-time feeds and (with a FIRMS MAP_KEY) the area API. Weather: NASA POWER daily (MERRA-2) for history, Open-Meteo forecasts for the next 16 days. Climate: NOAA Oceanic Niño Index.
  3. Quality control. Low-confidence detections, volcanoes, industrial heat sources and offshore flares are removed; sensor outages are detected so that “no data” is never read as “no fire”.
  4. Harmonization. A gradient-boosted (LightGBM, Poisson) transfer model learns, cell by cell and day by day over the 2012–2020 overlap, how many VIIRS S-NPP detections correspond to what MODIS saw (Terra and Aqua day/night counts, fire radiative power, pixel footprint, neighbouring days and cells, the cell's fire regime). Its daily pattern is rescaled to the monthly VIIRS/MODIS ratio over ±15 days. Accuracy is measured by cross-validation over whole years. NOAA-20/21 fill S-NPP gaps after rescaling.
  5. Expected vs most likely. The model estimates the expected VIIRS count, which is right for totals but smoother than any real day: it almost never says “no fire”. A season-aware quantile mapping, fitted on held-out years, turns it into the most likely count of the day, with the observed share of quiet days and extremes. The calendar and daily statistics use the most likely count; totals, 7-day anomalies, seasons and episodes use the expected value.
  6. Calendar. Day-of-year climatology pooled over ±15 days and all baseline years; anomalies as percentiles of the 7-day activity; fire-season onset (10%), peak and end (90%) per fire year starting at the quietest month; critical periods = the fewest calendar days holding 50% / 80% of normal activity.
  7. Forecast. Two LightGBM models per grid cell (chance of fire, expected detections) for 1–14 days ahead, driven by recent burning seen by all sensors, fire radiative power, the harmonized climatology, weather on the target day, the Canadian Fire Weather Index system (FFMC, DMC, DC, ISI, BUI, FWI), drought and ENSO. Area totals get empirical prediction intervals calibrated on unseen years. Each forecast day also shows the area's Fire Weather Index with its EFFIS danger class. A ridge model gives a 1–3 month outlook.

Limits

  • Hotspots are thermal anomalies at overpass time; clouds, smoke and canopy hide fires, and counts are not burned area.
  • The pre-2012 record is a model reconstruction with uncertainty (shown as bands and in the accuracy table); small fires that MODIS cannot see are estimated from the cell's regime.
  • Without a FIRMS MAP_KEY the public archive ends with the last complete year and the near-real-time feed only covers the last 7 days; the period in between is shown as “no data”. Add FIRMS_MAP_KEY to .env to fill it.
  • Forecast skill is measured with observed weather (an upper bound); real forecasts degrade with weather forecast error, mostly beyond 5–7 days. The lower bound (no weather forecast) is in the skill table.
